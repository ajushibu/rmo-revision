Chest drain lab

Start with the chest, add the bottles one at a time, then watch bubbling, swinging and suction behave. Simplified and not to scale. Pressures and volumes are for understanding, not for setting a real drain.

Why does air in the chest need a drain?

The lung is stretchy and always wants to shrink. It stays open only because the thin gap around it (the pleural space) has no air in it. Let air in and the lung springs back and falls away from the chest wall.

The drain is a one-way door: air can leave, nothing can come back in. Everything else on the next tabs is about keeping that one-way door working.

Why are there one, two or three chambers?

Add the chambers one at a time and push in fluid. Each chamber fixes a problem the one before it had.

Chambers

The water-seal depth is the push the lung must make for air to escape. About 2 to 3 cm of water is enough to let air out and stop air going back in. The fluid scale here is only illustrative.
