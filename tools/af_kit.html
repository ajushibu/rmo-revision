Atrial Fibrillation Study Kit

The Point of Care Medicine AF template, checked against Australian guidance, then turned into things you can click. Start on the first tab. A study aid, not a clinical decision tool.

Where Australia differs from the template

The template is US-based. Learn the right-hand column. Amber cells are the ones that would change what you do or write.

TopicUS templateAustralia (NHFA/CSANZ 2018)
Stroke risk scoreCHA₂DS₂-VASc. Anticoagulate at 2 or more in men, 3 or more in women.CHA₂DS₂-VA: female sex is not scored. Score 2 or more: anticoagulate. Score 1: consider. Score 0: do not.
Bleeding scoreHAS-BLED, but not in isolation.Do not use bleeding scores to withhold anticoagulation. Find and fix modifiable risks: uncontrolled hypertension, alcohol, NSAIDs, antiplatelets, anaemia, labile INR.
Choice of anticoagulantDOAC first line; edoxaban listed; apixaban "best choice" in CKD/ESRD.Apixaban, dabigatran or rivaroxaban in preference to warfarin. If CrCl is under 30 mL/min use warfarin. Warfarin also for moderate-severe mitral stenosis and mechanical valves. Cirrhosis: DOACs contraindicated. Edoxaban is not in the guideline.
Apixaban dose reduction2 of: age 80+, weight 60 kg or less, creatinine 1.5 mg/dL.2.5 mg twice daily if 2 of: age 80+, weight 60 kg or less, creatinine 133 µmol/L or more. (Same rule, Australian units.)
Rivaroxaban reduction15 mg if CrCl 15-50.15 mg daily if CrCl 30-49 mL/min (and below 30 means warfarin).
Dabigatran reduction110 mg twice daily if over 80 or high bleeding risk.110 mg twice daily if age 75+ and/or CrCl 30-50 and/or increased major bleeding risk.
AspirinNot for stroke prevention.Same. After ACS or stent, stop antiplatelets at 12 months and continue the anticoagulant.
Rate control drugsBeta-blocker or diltiazem/verapamil; digoxin second line.Same order. No diltiazem or verapamil with LV systolic dysfunction. IV verapamil with extreme caution. Amiodarone when reserve is marginal or in heart failure. Digoxin level aim under 1.2 ng/mL. Digoxin is slow (about 2 hours), so it is not for when you need the rate down fast. Amiodarone can also cardiovert, so with AF over 48 hours without anticoagulation it carries a stroke risk. These backup drugs are for ordinary AF only. Not for pre-excited AF (next row).
Pre-excited AF (delta wave on an old ECG, fast irregular wide rhythm)No AV-nodal blockers. Procainamide. Shock if unstable.Same rule: no beta-blocker, verapamil, diltiazem or digoxin, and avoid IV amiodarone too. Unstable: synchronised shock. Stable: procainamide is named by RACGP (the Australian Family Physician arrhythmia review); the Australian resus.com.au review lists IV procainamide or IV ibutilide. I could not confirm procainamide is stocked in Australian hospitals: use your hospital protocol and call your senior early. Do not mix this up with flecainide: in ordinary AF or flutter the AV-nodal blocker goes in first; in pre-excited AF none goes in at all.
Unstable AFSynchronised shock; 100 J biphasic.Urgent electrical cardioversion. Queensland ambulance protocol uses 100, 150, then 200 J. Follow your hospital's device and sedation protocol.
Drug cardioversionFlecainide, propafenone, amiodarone, dofetilide, ibutilide.Flecainide or amiodarone. Flecainide only without LV dysfunction, significant LVH or coronary disease, and give an AV-nodal blocker first.
After cardioversionAnticoagulate "at least 10 days".I could not retrieve the Australian figure. Standard teaching is at least 4 weeks, then continue by CHA₂DS₂-VA. Check eTG before you rely on either number.
How far to trust this tab. Scores, drug choice, dose-reduction rules and the 48-hour rule come from the 2018 NHFA/CSANZ guideline and its clinical fact sheet. The shock energies come from a Queensland Ambulance protocol, not a hospital guideline. I did not find a newer Australian AF guideline, but one may exist. Check eTG and your hospital's guideline before acting on any number.

Score the patient

Tick what applies. Both scores update, so you can see where the US and Australian rules disagree.

    Australian score: CHA₂DS₂-VA

    Score 0 and not anticoagulated: re-score every year. Age and new illness change it.

    US template: CHA₂DS₂-VASc

    The US rule counts female sex as 1 point and asks for 3 in women. Learn it so you understand US sources, but chart the Australian rule.

    Before you decide against anticoagulation for bleeding risk: a bleeding score alone is not a reason in the Australian guideline. List what you can fix first.

    Why it matters

    Cardioversion can restart organised atrial contraction and dislodge a clot sitting in the left atrial appendage, where about 90% of AF thrombi form. Without anticoagulation the stroke risk around cardioversion is quoted at 1-7%. That is why duration of AF decides the path.
