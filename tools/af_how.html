Five small experiments that show why rate control and drug cardioversion have the rules they do. Press the buttons and watch what changes. All numbers are illustrative, to show the idea. They are not doses or real patient data.

The AV node decides how many signals reach the ventricles

Blue ticks are signals fired by the atria. Black ticks are beats that got through to the ventricles, and your pulse is the black ticks. Ten seconds of rhythm.

Why give an AV-nodal blocker before flecainide

Atrial flutter: the atria fire about 300 times a minute. See what flecainide does to the ventricular rate.

Why flecainide needs a healthy ventricle

Flecainide slows electrical conduction everywhere, including in the ventricle.

Why the 48-hour rule exists

A quivering atrium does not empty properly, so blood pools in it. Choose how long the AF has been going and whether the patient is anticoagulated.

Why AV-nodal blockers are dangerous in pre-excited AF

This patient has a second electrical route from atria to ventricles (the accessory pathway, seen as a delta wave). The AV node has a speed limiter. The second route does not.
