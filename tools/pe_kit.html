Pulmonary Embolism Study Kit

Four parts of the Point of Care Medicine PE template that are hard to hold in your head, turned into things you can click. A study aid, not a clinical decision tool.

Answer three questions

The class moves as you change them. The starting answers are an example.

Cardiac arrest, or systolic BP under 90 mmHg not explained by low volume or an arrhythmia?

Right ventricular strain on echo or CT, or NT-proBNP above 500?

Troponin raised?

PESI is not calculated here: the template puts high risk at PESI IV or above, intermediate at III and low at I-II. Use a PESI calculator for the score.

Every class: oxygen to keep saturation above 90%, high-flow nasal oxygen if needed, and avoid positive-pressure ventilation.

      Step 3: D-dimer

      Only needed when PE is unlikely on Wells. Enter the patient's age and the lab value.

      The template also gives a second rule: below 500 with moderate pre-test probability, or below 1000 with low probability, rules out PE. Check which one your hospital uses and which units your lab reports.

      Next step

      Sources word the Wells cut-off differently: the book says a score under 4 with a negative D-dimer, the template uses low, moderate and high tiers, and the usual two-tier version is 4 or less (unlikely) vs above 4 (likely). This page uses the two-tier version. A patient who is unstable needs senior help and bedside echo, not a pathway.

          Left out on purpose

          Parts of the template that did not earn a picture or a drill, and why.

          Part of the templateWhy it is not here
          The ten trials (PEITHO, FLARE, iNOPE, SOME, CLOT, RE-COVER, AMPLIFY, EINSTEIN-PE, SELECT-D, PREPIC-2)Journal-club detail, not first-hour knowledge. The one lesson an RMO needs, that lysis is for preventing death in high-risk PE and brings bleeding, is built into the Risk class tab.
          ECG frequencies (normal in 20%, tachycardia 28%, T inversion V1-V4 7-14%, S1Q3T3 about 8%)One fact carries the value: a normal ECG does not exclude PE. A chart of four percentages adds nothing.
          Echo and CT signs of RV strain (McConnell sign, RV/LV above 0.9), Hampton hump, wedge infarctThese are image-recognition skills. Learn them from real images; a hand-drawn cartoon could mislead.
          Anticoagulant choice and duration (LMWH vs heparin, DOAC caveats, 3 months vs indefinite)Short rules that read fine as text, and doses must come from your hospital's guideline, not a study page.
          Risk factor odds ratiosA three-tier list that does not change what you do in the first hour.
          Virchow's triad, May-Thurner syndrome, PE in syncope, PICC and upper-limb DVT pearlsLow yield for a new RMO, and easy to recall without help.
